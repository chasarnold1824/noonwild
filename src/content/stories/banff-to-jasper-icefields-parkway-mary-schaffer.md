In the summer of 1907, a widow from Philadelphia came out of the mountains north of Lake Louise having failed at the one thing she had gone in to do. Mary Schäffer had spent four months on horseback with her friend Mollie Adams, a New York geography teacher, and two guides, Billy Warren and Sid Unwin, looking among other things for a large lake the Stoney Nakoda called Chaba Imne, Beaver Lake. They never found it. That season they met Samson Beaver, a Stoney man whose family Schäffer knew, and he sketched the route for her from memory. He had been to the lake as a boy, with his father, and had not been back in years.

Schäffer carried the map home to Philadelphia, and in June 1908 she rode north from Lake Louise again with the same party. Mollie Adams's diary records them crossing Bow Pass in snow on June 12, too deep to detour to the lookout over Peyto Lake. Later that summer they found it, a long lake walled by glaciers and peaks, and explored it on a raft they christened H.M.S. Chaba. Schäffer named Samson Peak, above the water, after the man who had drawn the map. She compared Lake Louise to a single pearl and this lake to a whole string of them.

She was not a trained explorer, and she said so. She had first come to the Rockies in 1889 with her husband Charles, a doctor and amateur botanist, painting and photographing wildflowers for his work. When he and both of her parents died in 1903, she came back alone to finish his book, and kept coming. In 1911 the Geological Survey of Canada asked her to go back and survey Maligne Lake, though she had no survey training, and her work helped bring the lake inside Jasper National Park. She settled in Banff, in a house she called Tarry-a-while, married her guide Billy Warren in 1915, and died there in 1939.

The route her pack train followed north is, give or take a valley, the Icefields Parkway, the road between Lake Louise and Jasper. It is the most beautiful drive in Canada, and most people do it far too fast. The honest answer is that it is not a day trip: the best of it is at the two ends and at one lake in the middle, and where you sleep decides how much of it you see. Here is how I would plan it, and where I would put you.

## How long should you spend on the Icefields Parkway?

Two nights at the least, and three if Maligne Lake is the reason you are going. The Parkway runs about 230 kilometres from Lake Louise to Jasper, and you could drive it in an afternoon. People do, and they arrive in Jasper having seen the Columbia Icefield through a windscreen.

The rule I use: one or two nights at the Lake Louise end, one night at Bow Lake, and two or three nights in Jasper. Bow Lake is about thirty minutes north of Lake Louise, close enough to feel like a short hop and far enough to be out of the day traffic by evening. From Jasper, Maligne Lake is 48 kilometres up its own valley, which is a morning in itself.

Drive north rather than south if you can. You end in Jasper, which is quieter, darker at night and a better place to linger than Banff, and you follow the line Schäffer took.

## When should you go?

June to early October for the trip most people picture, and late January to March for the one almost nobody takes.

**Summer and early autumn** is when everything is open. The Maligne Lake cruise to Spirit Island runs from May 28 to October 10 in 2027, and the Classic Cruise takes about an hour and a half. The Parks Canada shuttle to Moraine Lake ran from June 1 to October 12 this year, and Moraine Lake Lodge keeps a similar season, opening in early June and closing in early October. Glacier View Lodge at the Columbia Icefield was open from May 1 to October 12 in 2026. If you want all of it, June and September are the months I would choose: everything running, fewer people than July and August.

**Winter** is a different trip. The Parkway stays open, but Parks Canada is plain about it: there is no fuel anywhere along it, no cell service between the two ends, the road is not salted, and snow clearing runs from about 7 am to 3:30 pm. Winter tyres or chains are required from November 1 to April 1. Most hotels, restaurants and shops along the way close for most of the season. The Lodge at Bow Lake is the exception, open from late January to early April, and a winter night there, with the lake frozen and the road empty, is one of the best things in the Rockies. Maligne Lake in winter is for cross-country skiing and snowshoeing, not boats.

## Can you still see Maligne Lake the way Schäffer did?

Not quite, and that is the point of going early. The Classic Cruise crosses to Spirit Island, the small spruce-covered islet that is the lake's famous view. It is a good cruise. But Schäffer's lake was the whole of it, and the way to feel that is to be on the water when the first boats are still tied up.

Take the first departure of the day, or rent a canoe and paddle along the shore for an hour before the cruise traffic starts. Look for Samson Peak on the eastern side of the lake. Then walk the Mary Schäffer Loop, a short trail on the lakeshore that Parks Canada keeps open in winter too. It is not demanding, and it is the right way to end the morning.

## Do you need a reservation for Moraine Lake?

Yes, or a room at the lodge. Moraine Lake Road is closed to personal vehicles all year. You arrive on a Parks Canada shuttle from the Lake Louise Park and Ride, on a licensed tour, or as a registered guest of Moraine Lake Lodge. Parks Canada releases 40 per cent of shuttle seats in mid-April and the rest at 8 am two days before each departure, and a free Lake Connector shuttle runs between Lake Louise and Moraine Lake through the season.

The hotel guest's version is better: you drive in, and you have the lake before the first shuttle and after the last one.

## Where should you stay?

These are the properties I would build the trip around, from south to north. A note on ratings: Forbes Travel Guide currently rates none of them, so the useful mark here is the MICHELIN Key. Three of the five hold one.

### At Lake Louise: Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise

<figure><img src="https://www.chateau-lake-louise.com/content/uploads/2025/08/CLL_Product_Chateau_MountainsBehind_2200x1467.jpg" alt="Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise on the shore of Lake Louise, with the Victoria Glacier and mountains behind." loading="lazy"><figcaption>Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise. Image courtesy of <a href="https://www.chateau-lake-louise.com/press/fairmonts-celebrated-mountain-resorts-in-western-canada-recognized-with-michelin-key-distinction/" rel="noopener">Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise</a>.</figcaption></figure>

**Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise** is the grand railway hotel on the shore of the lake, and it holds a MICHELIN Key: the obvious choice, and for once the obvious choice is right. Hospitality has stood on this spot since 1890, and today it is a big house, 539 rooms and suites by Fairmont's own count, set at the eastern end of the lake with the Victoria Glacier across the water. What it has that nothing else does is the lake itself, at the door, before the day visitors arrive, with canoes in summer. Since September 2025 it has also had BASIN Glacial Waters, a thermal bathing circuit. Eat at Fairview, where afternoon tea is served looking down the lake, or at the Walliser Stube for fondue; the Lakeview Lounge is the place for a drink at dusk. The rooms to ask for are the Lakeside rooms, and if the budget stretches, one of the signature suites, such as the Belvedere or the Tom Wilson. Let our advisors quote current rates, which swing hard between a February weekday and an August Saturday. Book it for a first visit, for children, and for anyone who wants Lake Louise at seven in the morning.

### The quieter choice: Post Hotel & Spa

**Post Hotel & Spa** is the other answer at Lake Louise, down in Lake Louise village, and it also holds a MICHELIN Key. It is a Relais & Châteaux member, since 1990, with fewer than a hundred rooms, suites and cabins. The building began in 1942 as a simple lodge; from 1978 two Swiss brothers, André and George Schwarz, turned it into the most European hotel in the Rockies, and since 2021 it has belonged to Canadian Rocky Mountain Resorts. The reason people return is the cellar: more than 25,000 bottles and 3,800 selections, with a Wine Spectator Grand Award every year since 2002. Dinner is in The Dining Room, drinks in the Sir Norman Lounge, and in winter there is a Fondue Stübli; the Temple Mountain Spa has a saltwater pool. MICHELIN singles out the library, with rolling ladders and stone fireplaces. Ask for Watson House or the Pipestone Cabin, on the river. It is open all year, about five minutes from the ski hill. Book the Post for a couple, for a second visit, or for anyone who cares more about dinner than the view from the bedroom.

### For Moraine Lake: Moraine Lake Lodge

<figure><img src="https://morainelake.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Accomodation-HERO-desktop.jpg" alt="Moraine Lake Lodge on the shore of Moraine Lake, with the Valley of the Ten Peaks behind, in Banff National Park." loading="lazy"><figcaption>Moraine Lake Lodge. Image courtesy of <a href="https://morainelake.com/accommodation/" rel="noopener">Moraine Lake Lodge</a>.</figcaption></figure>

**Moraine Lake Lodge** is the only place to sleep on Moraine Lake, and that is the whole case for it. It has 33 rooms in all: 18 cabins, six Wenkchemna Rooms and nine rooms in the main lodge, designed in 1988 by the Canadian architect Arthur Erickson, and in the same owners' hands since 1991. It is unrated, and it does not need a rating. Because Moraine Lake Road is closed to private cars, guests are the only people who can drive in, which means you have the lake in the early morning and the evening, when the shuttles have stopped and the Valley of the Ten Peaks goes quiet. Breakfast, afternoon tea, guided hikes, naturalist talks and canoes are included; dinner is in the Walter Wilcox Dining Room. Ask for a Lakeshore Cabin, with a king bed, a river-rock wood-burning fireplace and a balcony over the water. Guests must be 16 or older, and the lodge is open only from early June to early October. Let our advisors quote rates and, more importantly, find you a night; there are not many. Book one night here, not three. It is an experience, not a base.

### Halfway: The Lodge at Bow Lake

<figure><img src="https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/63c8a9c0a95cd62f834920bb/dbc6165c-0b6e-4aac-a07d-2a61207e9337/Beyondtheroom-webres.jpg?format=2500w" alt="The Lodge at Bow Lake, the former Num-Ti-Jah Lodge, on the shore of Bow Lake on the Icefields Parkway." loading="lazy"><figcaption>The Lodge at Bow Lake. Image courtesy of <a href="https://viola-porpoise-7ef9.squarespace.com/stay" rel="noopener">The Lodge at Bow Lake</a>.</figcaption></figure>

**The Lodge at Bow Lake**, the old Num-Ti-Jah Lodge, is the one I would not skip, and the one that connects most directly to Schäffer. It sits on the shore of Bow Lake, about thirty minutes north of Lake Louise, and it holds a MICHELIN Key. It was built by Jimmy Simpson, the outfitter and guide who arrived in these mountains in 1897; it was Simpson who told Schäffer's party about the mysterious lake to the north, and her 1908 pack train passed this shore on its way over Bow Pass. He started the main lodge in 1937. It reopened in August 2023 under new ownership, renamed and renovated, with Chris Parnell as head chef. There are now 35 rooms: 17 in the historic lodge and 18 in a new Chalet that opens with the winter 2027 season. All meals are included: breakfast, a packed lunch for the trail, afternoon snacks and a four-course dinner at 6:30 in the Elkhorn Dining Room, at long shared tables by the fire, with drinks beforehand in Jimmy's living room, the lounge named for Simpson. There is no cell reception. Ask for the Num-Ti-Jah Suite at the top of the historic lodge: 50 square metres, a Japanese soaking tub and windows on three sides over the lake; the Bow Glacier Suites in the Chalet, 60 square metres, suit a family of four. Rates start at C$795 a night for two in winter 2027 and C$995 in summer 2027, with a two-night minimum. This is where the trip slows down, and it is the night people remember.

### At the Columbia Icefield: Glacier View Lodge

**Glacier View Lodge** is not a luxury hotel, and I would not pretend it is. It is the only place to sleep at the Columbia Icefield, about an hour south of Jasper, and its point is one thing: rooms that look straight at the Athabasca Glacier. Ask for a Glacier View room, not a Mountain View. The all-inclusive package adds an evening tour of the Columbia Icefield Skywalk and a morning Ice Explorer ride onto the glacier, after the day crowds and before them, and dinner is at Altitude on the second floor. It was open from May 1 to October 12 this year. Book it as a single night for families with children who want to stand on a glacier, not as a substitute for Bow Lake.

### In Jasper: Fairmont Jasper Park Lodge

<figure><img src="https://retail-insider.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/JPL-Lac-Beuvert-Aurora-scaled.jpg" alt="The northern lights over Lac Beauvert and Fairmont Jasper Park Lodge in Jasper National Park." loading="lazy"><figcaption>Lac Beauvert at Fairmont Jasper Park Lodge. Image courtesy of <a href="https://retail-insider.com/retail-insider/2026/06/fairmont-jasper-park-lodge-unveils-100m-transformation/" rel="noopener">Fairmont Jasper Park Lodge</a>.</figcaption></figure>

**Fairmont Jasper Park Lodge** is the place to end, and it has just been remade. It spreads across 700 acres on the shore of Lac Beauvert, and in June 2026 it unveiled a CAD $100 million renovation of the whole resort: more than 330 of its 397 rooms, suites and Signature Cabins redone, a new arrival and lakeside patio, and the Stanley Thompson golf course restored. The lodge came through the July 2024 wildfire with damage to two guest cabins and some staff buildings, and reopened that October. It holds neither a MICHELIN Key nor a Forbes rating, which says more about timing than about the hotel. There are three new restaurants: Lume, in the old Emerald Lounge, for garden-led plates and cocktails; Elderwood, in the timber-framed former Great Hall; and The Barbicon, a steakhouse built around the Main Lodge's original Tyndall stone fireplace. What it has that no other hotel on this route has is the dark: Jasper is a Dark Sky Preserve, and the lodge runs guided stargazing, along with a Glacial Plunge and sauna on the lake. Ask for one of the Signature Cabins, Outlook Cabin or Point Cabin, for a family or a group; for a couple, a renovated lakeside room. Let our advisors quote current rates. It is the best base for Maligne Lake, 48 kilometres from Jasper up its own valley, and the right place to spend the last two nights.

## A three-night plan, south to north

**Night one, Lake Louise.** Arrive from Calgary, walk the lakeshore in the late afternoon, dinner at the Post or the Chateau. If it is summer, take an early shuttle to Moraine Lake the next morning, or stay at the lodge.

**Night two, Bow Lake.** Drive the thirty minutes north by lunchtime. Walk the lakeshore, drive up to look down on Peyto Lake from the lookout Schäffer's party could not reach in the snow, and be back for dinner at 6:30.

**Night three, Jasper.** Leave Bow Lake after breakfast, stop at the Columbia Icefield, and reach Jasper by late afternoon. The next morning, the first boat on Maligne Lake.

## The lake at the end of the map

What strikes me about the Maligne Lake story is how little of it belongs to Schäffer alone, and how readily she said so. She found the lake with a map drawn by a Stoney man from a boyhood memory, guided by two outfitters, after a third told her it was there. Then she named the peak above the water for the man who drew the map. The lake was never lost. It simply had not been written down. Take the first boat, look for Samson Peak, and remember who drew the way.
