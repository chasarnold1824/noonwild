# Story format and voice

Modeled on Noon Travel's stories (noontravel.com/stories), e.g. "The Best Wellness Resorts in the U.S. Right Now.", "Hawaii Luxury Travel Guide 2026: Which Island, Which Hotel.", "What to Know Before Your First Aman Stay." Noon Wild adds one thing Noon does not have: every story is anchored in a true explorer story.

## Title

Search-phrase titles that promise a decision or a plan. Patterns:
- How to plan a trip to [place] like [person]
- How to visit [place], [the surprising hook]
- [Place] Travel Guide [year]: Which [X], Which Hotel
- What to Know Before Your First [experience]
- The [surprising true story], and how to see it now

## Front matter

- `dek`: one sentence, the hook plus the promise.
- `category`: Destination guide, Explorer stories, or Expert advice.
- `keyTakeaways`: 3 to 5 bullets, each a complete, specific line (a verdict, a rule, a season).
- `faq`: 4 or 5 questions real travelers search ("Is X worth it?", "When is the best time?", "How many days?", "Where do you fly into?", "Can you visit Y?"). Short, direct answers.
- `related`: slugs of 2 to 4 published Noon Wild stories.
- `sources`: every source in the fact table.

The template adds the full-bleed hero with credit, the advisor byline, "In this article" (from the H2s), Share links, Key Takeaways, the FAQ titled "What You Actually Want to Know", the advisor close and Further reading automatically. Do not write those into the body.

## Body (1,500 to 2,000 words)

1. **Intro, 3 to 4 short paragraphs: the story.** Open in the moment ("In the summer of 1930, Ernest Hemingway drove west..."). Who, where, what surprising thing happened, one vivid detail. End with a one-line pivot to the trip: "Ninety years on, his corner of Yellowstone is still the quiet one. Here is how to travel it."
2. **No pull quotes.** Never repeat a line from the body as a block quote (Chas's rule). Quotes stay inline in the paragraph.
3. **H2 sections, 4 to 6.** Alternate Noon's two heading styles: questions ("When should you go?", "Is the Lamar Valley worth it in winter?") and "Place: angle" ("Cooke City: the town at the end of the road"). Typical run: why this place, when to go, where to stay, the itinerary, what the explorer would still recognize.
4. **Hotels.** Bold name, then specs in one breath: "**Sage Lodge**, in Pray, Montana, has 38 rooms on the Yellowstone River and three MICHELIN Keys." Then the angle, who it is for, and a verdict line ("Book it when..."). Two or three per story. Mention price only if sourced ("Rates start around...").
5. **Itinerary.** Bold day labels: "**Days 1 and 2: Paradise Valley.**" One to three sentences each.
6. **Close** with a short reflective section tying the explorer to the traveler. No "In conclusion."

## Voice

- Confident, opinionated, second person. Short declaratives and the occasional fragment. "Five islands, five completely different trips."
- Verdicts and rules: "If it's your first time, fly into Maui." "One island per week of trip."
- Reframes: "Wellness travel used to mean a facial and a robe. It doesn't anymore."
- Signposts: "Here is what to actually know." "Here is how to travel it."
- Specific over general: names, numbers, months, room counts.
- "We" means Noon Wild's advisors; use it sparingly, mostly near the end.
- Warm about the explorer, never romanticizing colonialism or trophy hunting; say plainly when something has changed ("The bears are protected now").
- No em dashes (house rule for Noon Wild, unlike Noon). No exclamation marks. No "nestled", "hidden gem", "bucket list", "unforgettable", "breathtaking".
