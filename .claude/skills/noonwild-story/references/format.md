# Story format and voice

Modeled closely on Noon Travel's stories (noontravel.com/stories), studied November 2026: "How to Plan a Honeymoon: When to Book, How to Split the Trip, and the Hotels I Send Couples To.", "Sydney or Melbourne: Which to Book This Australian Summer, and Where to Stay in Each.", "White Elephant Aspen Is Open, and It's Already the Best New Hotel in the Rockies.", "Hawaii Luxury Travel Guide 2026: Which Island, Which Hotel." Before writing, re-read two of Noon's newest stories; their voice drifts and ours should follow it.

Noon Wild's one addition: every story is anchored in a true explorer story. Everything else should read like Noon.

## Titles

Long, specific, search-shaped, often a question and its answer, and they end with a full stop.
- How to Plan a Gorilla Trek in Rwanda: Permits, Seasons, and the Lodges Worth the Climb.
- Yellowstone Like Hemingway: Where He Stayed, When to Go, and Where to Stay Now.
- Svalbard or Antarctica: Which Expedition to Book This Year, and on Which Ship.
- [Lodge] Is Open, and It's Already the Best Safari Camp in [Region].
- What to Know Before Your First Luxury Safari Camp.

## Categories and how deep to go on hotels

Noon does not review hotels in every story. Match the depth to the category:

| Category | What it is | Hotel depth |
|---|---|---|
| Destination guide | Where to go, when, and where to stay | Full: 3 to 6 properties, each a dense paragraph (see "The property paragraph") |
| Hotels | One property, Noon's "White Elephant Aspen" format | Deepest: the whole story is the property, in sections (design, rooms and which suite, food and drink, what it unlocks, rates) |
| Explorer stories | The explorer's tale first, the trip second | Light: name 1 or 2 places to stay in a sentence or two each |
| Expert advice | How to plan something (permits, seasons, first safari) | Medium: pairings or a short "where I would put you" list |

Mix the calendar so roughly half of stories carry real hotel depth and half are story-led.

## Structure

1. **Opening, 3 or 4 paragraphs.** In the Noon voice, start from a real, recurring moment ("Every November the same question lands in my inbox...") or, for Noon Wild, from the explorer's moment ("In the summer of 1930, Ernest Hemingway drove west..."). State the honest answer early. End the opening by saying what the piece will do: "Here is how I would decide, and where I would put you."
2. **Optional TL;DR line** for Hotels stories: "TL;DR: [property] opened [date] on [street]: [rooms], [restaurant], [one distinctive thing]..."
3. **H2 sections, 4 to 7.** Mostly real questions people search ("When should you go?", "Do you need a permit?", "Which lodge should you book?"), with a few "Place: angle" headings. Answer each question in its first sentence, then explain: "Nine to twelve months before you travel if the lodge is small, seasonal or famous..."
4. **Where to stay** (when the category calls for it): property paragraphs, grouped if useful ("The classic", "The quieter choice", "The newer trio").
5. **Close** with a short reflective paragraph or an honest verdict. No "In conclusion".

The template adds the hero with credit, byline, "In this article", Key Takeaways, the FAQ ("What You Actually Want to Know"), the advisor close and Further reading. Do not write those into the body.

## The property paragraph (Noon's pattern)

One dense paragraph per property, packed with checkable specifics in this rough order:
1. Name, then what it is in one clause and its rating: "**Park Hyatt Melbourne** is the only Forbes Four-Star hotel in the city for 2026, and it is the old-money choice:"
2. Size and exact location: room and suite count, street or setting, what it sits next to.
3. What makes it unique: the design or architect, the building's story, the view, the signature experience nobody else has (before-hours ski runs, a private gorilla family briefing, a hide over a waterhole).
4. Food and drink by name: the restaurant, the chef, the bar.
5. The room to ask for, with its size: "The Terrace Rooms, with 530 square feet of private terrace, are the ones to ask for."
6. Practical: rates ("Rates start at $1,995 per night"), airport or park-gate distance, season it closes.
7. The verdict: who it is for. "For a first night in Melbourne, nothing else gives you the city like this."

Be honest where Noon is honest: "It is not on the Forbes 2026 list either, whatever its own website says." Say when something is unrated, closed for the season, or not worth the premium.

Every fact in the paragraph comes from the property's own site, Forbes Travel Guide, the MICHELIN Guide, or reputable travel press, and goes in `sources`.

## Voice

- **First-person advisor**, as Noon writes it: "I", "the pairings I use", "where I would put you". Use opinions and preferences freely. **Never invent experience** the author does not have: no "I have planned hundreds", no "my clients always". Stick to judgments and facts.
- Calm, confident, specific. Longer, well-built sentences than a blog, with short ones for verdicts: "No. Give it a day, ideally two."
- Rules of thumb stated as rules: "Two stops for anything from ten nights, one stop for anything under eight."
- Specific over general: dates, room counts, square metres or feet, minutes from the airport, the exact season.
- Honest framing: "the honest answer is...", "which surprises people", "that is the part people underestimate".
- "We" and "Noon Wild's advisors" near the end, sparingly.
- Warm about the explorer, never romanticizing colonialism or trophy hunting; say plainly what has changed.
- No em dashes (house rule; Noon's newest stories avoid them too). No pull quotes repeating the body. No exclamation marks. Avoid "nestled", "hidden gem", "bucket list", "unforgettable", "breathtaking".

## Length

- Destination guide and Hotels: 2,000 to 3,500 words.
- Explorer stories and Expert advice: 1,500 to 2,500 words.

## Front matter

- `dek`: one or two sentences, the hook plus the promise.
- `keyTakeaways`: 3 to 5 complete, specific lines (a verdict, a rule, a season, a price).
- `faq`: 4 or 5 questions people actually search, answered directly ("February 3, 2026. It is fully open and taking reservations now.").
- `related`: slugs of 2 to 4 published Noon Wild stories.
- `sources`: every source in the fact table.
